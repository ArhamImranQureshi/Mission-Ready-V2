    The process

    Every file.
    Every time.

    Our process comes from 35 years in contract and compliance work, where a file either passes review or it doesn't. There is no "mostly complete."

    Our promiseIf a document is missing, the clinician doesn't deploy. We'd rather call you about a delay than send you a liability.
    1. STEP 1

      Screen

      Structured interview, clinical skills checklist and a fit check against your unit.

      Interview · Skills checklist · Specialty match
    2. STEP 2

      Verify

      Licenses and certifications confirmed at the primary source, never from a photocopy.

      Nursys · State CNA registry · BLS/ACLS
    3. STEP 3

      Clear

      Background, drug screen, health records and federal exclusion checks.

      Background · Drug screen · TB/titers · OIG · SAM · I-9
    4. STEP 4

      Audit

      A second reviewer checks the full file against your facility's and contract's requirements, then signs off.

      Two-person review · Requirement matrix · Sign-off
    5. STEP 5

      Deploy

      Confirmed shift, orientation packet, and a check-in before the clinician arrives.

      Shift confirmation · Orientation · Pre-shift call
    6. STEP 6

      Sustain

      Expirations tracked and renewed ahead of time, with facility feedback after every assignment.

      Expiry alerts · Feedback loop · Re-audit

    Concept notes: not part of the live site

    Brand kit, v0.1

    Everything above is draft website copy you can use. Below are the pieces behind it: taglines, boilerplate, voice, the visual system and motion rules, plus the details I need from you before launch.

    Tagline options

    • Cleared before they clock in.Recommended. Leads with the audit-ready promise and works for facilities and the VA.
    • Every shift covered. Every file complete.Plain and operational. Good for proposals and LinkedIn.
    • Precision staffing. Uninterrupted care.Leans into the military heritage. Good as a sub-line under the logo.
    • Report ready.Short, veteran vernacular. Use for recruiting ads aimed at nurses.

    Boilerplate

    One-liner

    Mission Ready Medical Staffing is a service-disabled veteran-owned agency placing fully credentialed, audit-ready RNs and CNAs with long-term care, hospital and VA facilities.

    About

    Mission Ready Medical Staffing provides Registered Nurses and Certified Nursing Assistants to long-term care facilities, assisted living communities, hospitals and VA medical centers on a per diem, contract and travel basis. Founded by a 100% service-disabled veteran with 35 years of executive contract and compliance experience, Mission Ready verifies and audits every clinician file before deployment. A lean, digital operation keeps overhead low, delivering competitive bill rates to facilities and competitive pay to nurses.

    Voice

    Precise

    Say exactly what we do and when. Name the checks, the sources and the timelines. Specifics build trust faster than adjectives.

    Accountable

    Make promises we can keep, and own it when we can't. "We'll call you about a delay before we send an incomplete file."

    Respectful

    Military discipline without barked orders. Nurses are professionals and facility managers are busy, so write to both like peers.

    Say

    Audit-ready · Verified at the source · Cleared to deploy · Covered · Accountable · Continuity of care · Report ready

    Avoid

    "Rockstar nurses" · "Best-in-class" · "Synergy" · Combat metaphors ("boots on the ground," "battle") · Any claim we can't document

    Near-black gives the brand a modern, high-contrast edge. Emerald is the single action color for buttons, "verified" states and key words. Silver, a nod to dog tags, is for secondary details. Keep emerald scarce so it always means "go."

    Public Sans comes from the U.S. Web Design System that federal sites use, which gives it quiet credibility with VA buyers. For motion, the scroll effects all tell one story: a file moving from pending to cleared. Chevrons rise, checks tick, a stamp lands. Visitors who turn on reduced motion get the finished, static page.

    Before launch: what I need from you

    • Founder name, branch of service and photo for the "Why Mission" section.
    • SBA VetCert (SDVOSB) status. Don't claim set-aside eligibility on the site until you're certified. The copy is marked where this applies.
    • SAM.gov registration, UEI and CAGE code for the capabilities snapshot.
    • The states you'll launch in, and any staffing-agency registrations or licenses those states require.
    • Pay practices to advertise (weekly pay, benefits, travel housing stipend) so the nurse section can make concrete promises.
    • Phone, email, domain and legal entity name for the footer and contact paths.
    • Accreditation plans, such as Joint Commission Health Care Staffing Services certification, which many hospitals look for.
